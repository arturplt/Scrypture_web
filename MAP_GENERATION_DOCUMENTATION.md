# Map generation

Three kinds of world (`World.kind`, chosen in the world list as Flat / Hills / Lake District):

- **procedural** (flat): `worldgen.ts` `generateChunk`: biomes and water from noise, no height.
- **lakedistrict**: a real map (`lakedistrict.ts`, `lakeLoader.ts`), heights from a corner grid.
- **terraced** ("Hills"): an infinite Godus-style stepped height field, described below. The to-do list for it is [ROADMAP_TERRACE.md](ROADMAP_TERRACE.md).

## The terraced world

Everything is a pure function of (seed, x, y), in `src/domain/terrace.ts`.

- **Elevation** (`elevation`) is in *layers* (below 0 is the sea): a domain warp, a slow "continent" field, big hills (with a ridged octave for crests), rolling ground and a small wiggle so the edges never trace the noise lattice. The slow terms (`lowAt`) are worked out on a coarse lattice and blended; the quick ones (`detailAt`) at every point. The lattice hash is one multiply-xor chain and one avalanche (`hash01`).
- **Inland lakes** (`LAKE_SCALES`, `lakeOfCell`, `lakeProbe`, `terraceLakeAt`, `terraceLakeShoreAt`): three sizes, each on its own grid of coarse cells (160, 80 and 40 tiles) with at most one lake a cell and a slow wetness noise raising or lowering the odds, so they gather in some regions and leave others dry: big ones (16 to 34 tiles' radius), middling (8 to 17) and small tarns (3 to 7), about 1 to 2% of the land (less on seeds that are mostly sea or dry). A lake fills a basin: its level is the lowest shelf on a ring of ground just outside it (where it would spill), a cell tries up to four sites and takes the first whose ring is not steep (at most 5 layers apart) and not near the sea, so a lake is never perched on a slope. The water is a rough disc (rim wobbled by noise) inset one step below its shore, so a low wall of one layer rings it, standing exactly on tile edges (`detailAt`: a grid point that touches only water tiles is at the water's level, one on the edge is on the shore's layer line), so the tiles drawn as water are the low ones and no water lies on the upper level; round that is a shore of level ground two and a half tiles wide (sand), and past that the elevation blends back into the land over 6 to 20 tiles (growing with the lake), so the ground falls away in a run of terraces and there is always level ground before any drop. Where influences overlap, the lake with most weight wins. Pure in (seed, x, y), laid into `detailAt`, so the grids, tiles, maps and far mesh agree; the spawn never lands in one.
- **Stepped height** (`terraceH`): each layer is a level shelf (`TERRACE_STEP` = 0.45 tiles a layer) and the step to the next is a vertical wall, a right angle, standing on the elevation's contour. The sea and the lowest shelf are one flat sheet at 0.
- **Grids** (`TerraceField.grid`): a chunk's elevation at every half tile (33 x 33 points), cached (900 grids). The elevation is *opened* (eroded then dilated by `OPEN_R` = 2 points) on a 4-point margin round the chunk, which shaves off every bump narrower than 5 points (2.5 tiles): without it, wherever the smooth field just touches a layer's line there is a tiny plateau, a little block on the shelf. Neighbouring chunks work out the shared margin the same way, so they agree. A closing after it (`FILL_PITS`) fills the pits as narrow. The wider margin makes a grid cost about 1.0 ms (0.55 with the opening alone; it was 0.23 before either). Set `FILL_PITS` to false to go back to the cheaper one. `heightAt` is `terraceH` of the elevation read linearly in the grid's triangles (each cell cut top-left to bottom-right), which are the triangles the chunk mesh is cut from, so the plane, the sprites and the ground agree. The grids are warmed a few at a time ahead of the plane (`prewarm`). The maps read through a second, small field of the same seed (`World.mapField`), so they never push the game's grids out of the cache.
- **Tiles** (`worldgen.ts` `generateTerraceChunk`): water below the sea, sand on layer 0 and on the beach shelf, `terraceBiome` from the layer and the region's moisture (`terraceMoist`), `LEDGE_BIT` on tiles next to another layer (nothing stands on a ledge).
- **Far view** (`terraceFarMesh`, `TERRACE_FAR_LODS`): three coarse meshes round the eye, built a few rows a frame (a generator), rebuilt as the eye travels. The finest follows the terraces (`terraceH`); the others use the smooth elevation. The renderer uploads them a slice a frame, into the same buffers, with one shared index buffer a size.
- **Water the weather makes** (`Ecosystem.sinkWater`, called from `put` whenever a tile turns to water or back): on the terraced hills every such tile (a flood, a beck, a tarn, a pond) is sunk into the ground, and the more water there is round a tile the deeper: one step below the shelf it stands on if it has dry neighbours, two if water is on all eight sides, three if on all sixteen beyond as well. The surface is always the shelf beside it, so it gets the same see-through sheets as a lake, and its tile is drawn as sand under them (only above the lowest shelves). A flood that grows or a tarn that fills therefore deepens towards its middle. The tiles are blurred into one smooth field (`TerraceField.blurAt`: a gaussian of sigma half a tile, `FILL_SIGMA`; the rim is where it crosses `FILL_RIM`), and the ground eases down to the bed across the rim, so a lone tile is a small round pool, a row of tiles (a beck) one smooth channel with rounded bends, and touching tiles run together, instead of square pits; the sheets follow the same rim. A beaver's dam pond (tiles made under the `dam` cause, tracked in `Ecosystem.damWater`) is treated the same way but marked as its own kind (`setFill`'s `kind`, blurred like the depth, `waterSheetAt` adds a half to the surface layer), and the shader tints its sheets a murkier green. Sinking works from the second shelf (layer 1; the bed is then layer 0); on the beach shelf (layer 0), which cannot be sunk below the sea, the water is a thin film sheet (0.18 of a layer) over the ground with the same smooth rim, so no weather-made water is left as a flat tile picture (only the map's own sea is). A world loaded with changes sinks and sheets the water it already has (`Ecosystem.restoreWater`).
- **Far biomes**: `terraceFarMesh` colours each vertex by the biome of the land there (`terraceBiome` of its layer and `terraceMoist`, now defined in terrace.ts and re-exported by worldgen): sand for dry country and the bare tops, a darker green for the woods, a cooler green for the wetland, meadow green elsewhere, each multiplied by the layer's tint.
- **Far contours**: the smooth far levels get their layers back in the fragment shader (`uFar` branch of `FS`): a thin dark line wherever the height crosses a whole layer, fading out once the bands are thinner than a few pixels, so no mesh has to be cut into steps.
- **Colour**: `BAND_STOPS` is the one table of grass tints by layer; `bandTint` (maps, far mesh) reads it and `bandGlsl()` writes the shader's copy.

### Weather

`Ecosystem.slopes` is true on the Lake District and the terraced hills: rain runs downhill (`shed`, `runoff` follow `world.metresAt`, the smooth elevation times `TERRACE_LAYER_M`) and wets the ground it crosses. On the Lake District a steady line of it cuts a beck on a level shelf (`beckSpot`); on the terraced hills a tile crossed `STREAM_PATH_AT` (6) times makes a stream of its whole way (`connectStream`): the path the runoff itself takes, downhill, to water (the sea, a lake, a tarn, another beck) or to a hollow (which is given a small pool), becomes one beck, at least 6 tiles, where it crosses a ledge it falls over it, so the wet ground and the water join into a river that runs into the reservoir. A sea keeps its water (it feeds clouds but does not dry). `Ecosystem.hilly` (the Lake's corner grid, rivers widening, beaver dam ponds) is still the Lake District only. Tarns work on both: on the terraces a tarn is a closed shelf (`tarnAt`: no way down, 6 to 40 tiles), filled to the shelf's top and no more. Water over the terraces is a flat sheet inset one step below the shelf its water has reached (tarns and becks too): `TerraceField.setFill` sets the flooded tiles to that level (the natural ground is kept, `naturalAt`, and ponds measure by that), and the chunks it touches have their meshes cut again (`takeDirty`, `WorldGL.dropTerrainMeshes`). Beaver dam ponds on the terraces still use the flat-world rules.

### Drawing (`worldgl.ts`)

Inland water has depth and a smooth edge. A lake's tiles are drawn as sand (`chunkDrawn`: the picture only; the world still counts them as water), so there is no tile picture with a harsh water edge. The water is see-through sheets cut from the same ground pieces as the bed (`terraceChunkMesh`: over each piece that lies under water, a copy at each layer from just over the bed up to the surface, `WATER_SHEETS_MAX` at most, drawn after the ground at 0.45 alpha with no depth write, in the water's own colour, shaded as water but without picture-based foam; the callback is `TerraceField.waterTopAt(x, y)`: fills by tile, lakes by their smooth rim), so a lake's edge is the smooth curve of the bed's own wall, and the top sheet is exactly at the height of the shore. The ground under water is marked in the vertex alpha and drawn as the sand picture darkened and shifted to teal. A lake's bed steps down towards its middle in rings (`deep` layers, one every `sw` tiles in from the rim, as a smooth slope cut along whole-layer lines: terraces like the hills', but negative); the ground eases across a tile's width centred on the rim, so the wall stands on the rim. The rim of a lake swells and narrows gently, so it is rounded. Beds on the lowest layers (and the sea) have no sheet.

A terraced chunk's mesh comes from `terraceChunkMesh` (terrace.ts): the grid's triangles are cut along the elevation's whole-layer contours into level pieces (each at its own shelf's height), and a vertical wall is stood on every cut. Elevation is linear inside a triangle, so a cut is a straight segment and the segments of neighbouring triangles join into the flowing edge; a triangle no contour crosses costs no extra vertices. The renderer (`prepTerrain`, `terraceMesh`) packs it with 32-bit indices. The `uTerrace` branch of the fragment shader draws band tints, sandy earth walls (tinted by the shelf above), and a light lip and dark foot on the walls. A wall over a water tile is an earth wall too, except in stretches picked by a hash where water lies on its high side: those are waterfalls (animated foam streaks). Without WebGL the 2D view tints each chunk and draws a dark contour where the layer changes (`terraceTinted` in `WorldMap.tsx`). The minimap and the full-screen map draw bands, light/dark relief that grows with zoom, and a stronger line every fifth layer (`terracePixel`).

### Checking it

`npx vitest run src/domain/terrace.test.ts`; micro-benchmarks `npx vite-node scripts/bench-terrace.mts` (grid build) and `scripts/bench-far.mts` (far mesh slices). Visual checks need WebGL: the built-in browser pane has none, use headless Chromium with SwiftShader (see ROADMAP_TERRACE.md).
